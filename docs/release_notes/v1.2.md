## Overview

v1.2 opens a new subsystem: **wind**, for terrestrial Arctic surface deployment — cold,
high wind, icing-prone. #82 asked for a chassis-level exploration of hosting modular wind
hardware, and that is where this lands, after a route that went through four distinct
concepts and two superseded designs.

The shape of the release is a **narrative of things being disproved**. Four times during
this work a plausible idea turned out to be wrong under analysis, and each correction
changed the design:

| Claim | Why it failed |
|---|---|
| "Ice is the tuning element" | A solid block has **no free surface**, so it contributes mass and no stiffness. Ice can never be a spring. |
| "Mass ratio is 14–69× above the useful 1–5% band" | Wrong arithmetic, and the wrong *rule* — that band applies to a stable structure needing damping. **This sail stands up**: it is an inverted pendulum, and the liquid's job is holding it up, not absorbing vibration. |
| "2-DOF pendulum tuned mass absorber" | Assumes a light absorber on a heavy structure. At μ ≈ 1.0 the eigenanalysis gives a positive eigenvalue. Implemented, it diverged to **1700°**. |
| "Seasonal self-tuning" | Ice growth is seasonal **destabilisation**. See R1 below. |

What survives is a **liquid-stabilised inverted pendulum**: a flat sail that screws itself
into frozen ground and is held upright by a water column, whose free surface supplies the
restoring stiffness. No latch is needed to keep it standing.

## Design progression — four concepts, two superseded

| Version | Concept | Fate |
|---|---|---|
| v1 `amu_wind_chassis_v1` | Wind mount on the full 17.92 × 12.0 AMU hull | **Superseded** — hull is orbital infrastructure, wrong for a ground site |
| v2 `amu_wind_anchor_v1` | Interlocked helical-pile ring, compression-only couplers | **Superseded** — the ring created a cascade-failure question that never went away |
| v3 `amu_wind_grove_v3` | Interlocked bladeless grove, self-erecting raked ring | **Superseded** — still needed the ring, still needed the cascade answer |
| v4 `amu_wind_sail_v4` | Portal-frame sail, water-column tuning | Superseded — pendulum framing was wrong |
| **v5 `amu_wind_sail_v5`** | **Vertical sail on stepped base, TVMD cells at the foot** | **Current** |

All four remain in `cad_and_blender/openscad/wind/` as the comparison set. The
interlocking thread — ring, couplers, screw jacks, cascade analysis — was **removed, not
deprecated**, because a flat sail with no structural linkage never asks the cascade
question.

## The blocking finding: it topples in winter

Ice grows **downward** from the cell top as a site cools. That shortens the water column,
which **both lowers μ** (less mass) *and* **raises μ_crit** (weaker stiffness). Same
direction twice.

| `ICE_TOP` | margin | `f_sail` | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 6.43× | 1.018 Hz | ✅ |
| **0.10** | **5.34×** | **0.911 Hz** | ✅ default |
| 0.20 | 3.47× | 0.687 Hz | ✅ |
| 0.30 | 2.00× | 0.438 Hz | ✅ |
| 0.40 | 0.94× | — | ❌ **topples** |
| 0.45 | 0.55× | — | ❌ **topples** |

**The design gets least stable in deep winter, when the wind is worst.** This is the
single most important result in the release and it is invisible in a kinematic animation —
it only appeared once real dynamics were integrated.

## 🐛 The bug worth knowing about

`cell_cassette()` called `translate([0,0,h]) damper_bay()` while `damper_bay()` **already**
placed cells at `+CELL_BAND_H`. The double offset put the cells at 0.624–1.24 instead of
0.004–0.62 — **above the cassette and up inside the blade.**

It was invisible in renders, because the blade is cut on the same side and the cells
stayed on screen. Every assembled view and animation since the cassette split showed them
floating in the wrong component.

Caught by **intersecting** the cassette against the blade and finding 154 facets of overlap
where there should be none — then validating the test with a control at `BLADE_GAP = −0.05`
(152 facets, correctly detecting real interference). The first control tried was invalid
and would have hidden the bug.

**Lesson: a visually plausible render is not a geometrically correct one.** Overlap has to
be proven absent, not eyeballed.

## Serviceability

The most frequent maintenance job on the machine — draining and refilling the damper
cells to re-tune — **required cutting the sail open**, because the cells were sealed inside
the shell with no access. The sail is now a swap-on-blade assembly:

```
lower flange plate (fixed to collar)
  → CELL CASSETTE    4 quick-release bolts, withdraws sideways
     → BLADE_GAP     0.035 service clearance + thermal break
        → SAIL BLADE  4 quick-release bolts, lifts straight off
```

Quick-release studs undo **by hand** — knurled collar, draw handles, no tools. That is the
entire point at a remote site in gloves at −40 °C.

## Transport: the envelope problem dissolved

The v1 chassis needed **scale 0.216** — it was 17.92 m long. Going flat changed the
problem entirely.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Assembled height | **2.595 m** |
| Nested panel footprint | 0.610 × 0.290 m → ~20 across a 20ft, 3 rows deep |
| Longest single item once split | **1.895 m** (blade alone) |
| **Scale needed** | **0.998** against a 20ft internal limit |

Met by geometry, not by a documented fudge factor. *Caveat:* 2.595 m against 2.59 m is a
5 mm margin, inside build tolerance — shipping blade and cassette separately removes the
question.

## Joint design — and what it is honestly not

The v1 mount-boss geometry was relocated to the sail foot, where the load actually is. But
it is **not** a tight flange, because a tight bolted flange and a joint that rotates 7° are
mutually exclusive. At the original bolt circle a 6° rotation drags the holes ~22 mm
sideways. So the jobs were split:

| Job | Carried by |
|---|---|
| Rotation | the pin, on the bearing race |
| Moment | the gusset cone, bearing on the collar spigot |
| Separation | the bolts, held in tension by preload |

Something at the joint has to give, and it is the pin.

## Rendering toolchain

- `scripts/render_sail_animation.sh` — sweeps `$t`, assembles MP4 via ffmpeg
- `--close` / `--wide` / `--az` / `--name` / `--def` for view control
- 48 frames @ 24 fps in ~17 s
- Preview mode default: 0.3 s/frame vs ~9 s for CGAL, **and it preserves model colours** — headless CGAL PNG export overrides every `color()` call with a theme palette

Three rendering bugs that produced plausible-looking wrong output are documented in the
script: camera arguments transposed (blank frames that ffmpeg happily assembled into a
valid 2.2 kB MP4), `$NAME_contact` read as a different variable, and `--cam` never having
taken effect because the presets ran after argument parsing.
